# M03 Physical actors and terrain in the actual game

Dependency: M02 verified and merged. Read the architecture's ownership, tick order and terrain sections. Deliver one playable adventure encounter using coherent Rapier motion, not a playground-only implementation.

## Steps

1. Migrate player and combat-enemy movement to intent-driven bodies. Start with the documented locked-rotation dynamic motor; preserve responsive acceleration, spirit, dash, invulnerability, stagger and external knockback. Remove duplicate actor movement/contact solving.
2. Generate stable terrain/decoration colliders from occupied world regions and tile patches. Wire patch edits, region loading, land transitions and reset/dispose into the registry. Camera visibility must not control physical existence.
3. Establish interaction groups for players, monsters, bosses, NPCs, props, terrain, loot and sensors. Add crowd contacts, prop blocking and appropriate swept checks to policies. Dashing can phase through actors while still respecting solid world obstacles.
4. Add basic obstacle-aware enemy steering and stuck recovery. Preserve the original combat damage rules and nine-area route even before advanced environmental damage exists.
5. Add explicit ambient-physics participation. Handover between ambient steering and Rapier happens once at a tick boundary, retains identity/position and never runs both integrators. Respect the selected population without hidden FPS-driven eligibility caps.
6. Feed solved poses to normal rendering and observers. Define the semantic physical-state envelope needed by M04. Keep unfinished shared physics behind the existing rollout boundary until replication is verified.

## Acceptance

- Real movement, held attack, Whorl, dash and enemy charge work in a normal encounter. Knockback survives the next AI/movement update; players do not spin or become permanently pinned.
- Patch an obstacle, traverse a chunk boundary, recall and enter a new land without ghost colliders or duplicate actors.
- Toggle actor contacts and ambient physics in two regions; verify each entity has one movement owner. No chosen ambient participant silently disappears to satisfy a benchmark.
- Functional run, saved-state validation and browser controls remain usable. Compare meaningful collision outcomes rather than requiring the old solver's exact trajectory.
